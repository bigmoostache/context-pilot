One agent per project

Not a coding tool that also
does other things.

Point an agent at a folder and hand it the work. Nothing in the system assumes what's in that folder — the same agent triages a support inbox, reviews a stack of leases, reconciles a dataset, or ships a feature. It sets up its own workspace, keeps going while you're elsewhere, and stops when it needs a decision from you.

  • Research Find out who else is doing this, and tell me what actually holds up.
  • Documents Draft the response, then check every clause against our own terms.
  • Data Reconcile these two exports and explain whatever won't match.
  • Ops Watch the queue overnight. Wake me only for the ones I have to decide.
  • Code Ship the feature, run the checks, and don't stop until they're green.

Same agent. Same system. Different folder.

The box

Everything above,
delivered plugged in.

A small fanless machine that holds your whole team of agents. It fits in a bag, draws less than a lightbulb, and needs nothing from you but a power socket. Turn it on and the agents are already there.

Your data stays on it

Agents, memory, history, files — all of it lives on the box, on your premises. No account to hand your work to, and nothing phones home.

It travels with you

Built-in 5G, and it broadcasts its own secured Wi-Fi. On a site with no network, your agents and your connection arrive in the same bag.

It joins your network

Back at the office it's one more machine on the LAN, reachable over your company VPN like anything else you run.

Plug and play

Power and go. Nothing to rack, nothing to configure, no IT department required to get the first agent working.

  • 5GOnline anywhere there's signal

Yours

No account. No telemetry.
Open core.

Run it yourself and Daharness keeps its state in your own folders. There is no analytics, no tracking, and no phone-home to switch off — the only thing that leaves is the model call, to the provider you picked.

It's built on Context Pilot, the open-source core, under the MIT license. Every file operation and every network call is there to read.
